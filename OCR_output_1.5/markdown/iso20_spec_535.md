Charging via the public and private infrastructure with the same communication protocol saves costs and contributes significantly to the acceptance of this document. But what does using the same charging protocol in PE mean in detail?

During the start of the charging process, the setting up of the TLS channel in PE should run in the same way. Although the process is the same, the EV can use the certificate chain of the PE EVSE to recognize that it is charging on a PE EVSE in a PE. The EV can then choose the supported application protocol for PE. It can consider the method with which identity it would like to act on the application layer in PE. EV and PE EVSE can then decide for PE whether or with which target setting, charge schedule the control runs and whether or not a re-scheduling is permitted in PE.

This shows that the EVCC securely recognizes a PE SECC if it wants to implement less than is necessary for the public charging functions. This means that an EVCC should be able to distinguish the trust anchor of a PE SECC from a public EVSE. When validating a leaf certificate in the TLS handshake, the EV can then clearly determine whether this is a leaf certificate from a public EVSE or not. The weak point in this case is that not only the PE SECC manufacturer or PE EVSE operator can issue his/her own PE root CA certificates but also an unknown and unauthorized attacker. An attacker could even try to be a public infrastructure for the EV in the PE.

If these boundary conditions are to be taken into account, however, different security requirements may arise:

Challenge 1: On the one hand, it is possible that an EV user is very interested at which PE EVSE his/her EV is charged, since his/her EV only allows a higher range of functions for trustworthy PE EVSEs. The PE EVSE owner also wants to know which EV is currently being charged on his/her PE EVSE. Although his/her PE EVSE is not publicly accessible, it may still not be protected well enough. The PE EVSE owner wants only trustworthy EVs to be able to be charged at his/her PE EVSE. EV and PE EVSE should trust each other, because with trust, both can allow more functionality. Certificates from both should be trustworthy. In order for PE EVSE and EV to validate each other's leaf certificate, they should have the correct root CA certificate from the other party. In this case, in particular, the users of the PE EVSE and the EV should be exempted from any certificate management, which considerably simplifies the commissioning and maintenance of the devices for the user. Manufacturers of PE EVSEs and EVs should renew the relevant certificate themselves. (Prerequisite: The manufacturers always have a secure connection to the PE EVSE).

Challenge 2: On the other hand, in contrast to the challenge 1, the manufacturer wants to reduce costs during or after the production of their devices. In addition to installing the own root CA certificate chain for the first time, the manufacturers of the devices may no longer want to deal with certificate management later and save the online connection to the devices. In this case, the manufacturer transfers the effort for certificate management of the devices to the user, who would then manage the required certificates himself. The manufacturers would then design the operation of the PE EVSE and the EV in such a way that the user can exchange the trust anchors before charging (e.g. provisioning mode CPM4PE). EV and PE EVSE should then securely transmit and store the required trust anchors at least once before the charging process can begin. Refer to H.2.2.3 for details of CPM4PE.
